Evidence base

What I used to understand the problem

SRS and HLD requirements, the Rosterix AI UX strategy, the UX research report, workforce scheduling research, AI trust patterns, enterprise UX considerations and accessibility requirements.

Research integrity

What the evidence does — and does not — claim

The research synthesises workflow friction across 6 core personas, business constraints, and live error logs before production rollout. It serves as an empirical foundation to safeguard user accountability rather than claiming unverified autonomy.

“Rosterix is a decision-making system where information becomes progressively fragmented as it moves through the organisation.”

01

Context arrives too late

Users often discover the information they need only after taking an action or receiving a rejection.

02

Memory lives in people

Recurring roster and tagging decisions rely heavily on personal knowledge and previous spreadsheets.

03

Problems surface downstream

Conflicts and payroll consequences can appear after the original workforce decision was made.

04

Trust must be earned

AI needs to propose and explain before it earns the right to automate higher-risk actions.

02

Define

Frame the real decision-making problems

The problem was bigger than roster creation. Rosterix required users to reconstruct context, detect risk and justify decisions manually across a fragmented workflow.

Problem 01

Context fragmentation

Relevant information is distributed across projects, employee records, roster history, approvals and payroll-related workflows.

Problem 02

Delayed feedback

Business-rule violations and downstream conflicts can become visible only after a submission or later process step.

Problem 03

Untrusted automation

A recommendation without explanation creates uncertainty about accountability and makes consequential AI harder to adopt.

How might we

How might we help every Rosterix user make faster, safer and more informed workforce decisions by bringing the right context and risk signals into the moment of decision — while keeping humans accountable for consequential actions?

01 • Context before action

Give users the information they need before they commit.

02 • Risk before submission

Move validation upstream into the decision itself.

03 • AI proposes, humans decide

Keep consequential actions reviewable and reversible.

04 • Explain the why

Make recommendations inspectable rather than magical.

North Star: Move Rosterix from a system that records workforce decisions to a system that helps users make better workforce decisions.

Iterate

Feed real-world evidence back into the product

The product does not end at launch. Every decision creates evidence that can improve the experience, the rules and eventually the AI.

OBSERVE→ MEASURE→ IDENTIFY→ HYPOTHESISE→ DESIGN→ VALIDATE→ RELEASE↺
Product learning

Repeated overrides reveal missing context

If managers repeatedly override a recommendation for the same operational reason, the product may be missing an explicit business rule or context signal.

AI learning

Decisions become feedback

Recommendation → user decision → override → downstream outcome can become a feedback loop for future model and UX improvements.

Trust learning

Autonomy must be earned

Progress from informing to bounded automation only when behavioural evidence supports the next level of autonomy.

What I would validate next

01 • Qualitative interviews

Run moderated interviews with real stakeholders to validate the pain-point matrix.

02 • Trust calibration

Usability-test the AI Assistant and explainability patterns for trust calibration.

03 • Design system scale

Extend the design system from the snapshot into complete component specifications before later recommendation-layer phases.

The final product direction is not “more AI.” It is better decisions — with the right context, the right risk signal, and the right amount of automation at the right moment.

REFLECTION

Reflection & Learnings

Accountability in Enterprise AI

The work changed how I think about AI in enterprise products. The opportunity was not simply to automate the most manual task. It was to understand where a decision becomes risky, what context the user needs to make that decision, and where the system must deliberately keep a human in control.

01

Automation is not the goal.

The most valuable AI moments are the ones that remove repetitive reconstruction of context while preserving the user's ability to inspect, edit and approve the result.

02

Trust is an interaction pattern.

Confidence indicators alone are not enough. Users need to see why a recommendation was made, what evidence supports it and what could happen if they accept it.

03

Enterprise UX is about relationships.

A roster change is rarely an isolated action. It can affect tagging, allowance, approvals and payroll. The interface has to expose those relationships at the moment they matter.

04

Design systems are part of the product strategy.

Once patterns such as validation, explainability, risk states and AI recommendations repeat across workflows, systemizing them becomes essential for consistency and implementation quality.

WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT

Validate the highest-risk workflows with Rosterix stakeholders and production evidence: measure recommendation acceptance, override behaviour, validation catches, approval turnaround, accessibility issues and downstream payroll exceptions. Those signals would determine where Rosterix should remain assistive and where bounded automation could safely expand.
